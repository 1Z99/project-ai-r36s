227 TV / Field Manual

Know your atlas.

The controls, settings and boundaries of 227 TV: country-by-country browsing, fullscreen playback, an experimental four-view matrix, and saved files you can inspect and back up.

Controls

Read the current footer: buttons change meaning in search, menus and playback. These are user-facing actions on the tested R36S, not a universal controller mapping.

There is no separate fullscreen button or matrix-cell maximize action. Menus alone do not stop playback. Stop playback, B from the watch view, switching playback or exiting TV closes players; stale network results cannot restart a stopped session.

The three tabs

WORLD, FAVORITES and RECENT
TabWhat it holds
WORLDThe rotating atlas. Clicking a country starts an eased camera flight along the shortest longitude path, then pulls into close-up and reveals that country's channels with the Local AI Matrix glyph scramble, one row at a time. The selected country renders in amber with its channel-count numeral at the country's centroid.
FAVORITESStations you saved with Y. It opens from saved snapshots without downloading the world index.
RECENTUp to 50 stations selected for single-channel playback, newest first, from saved snapshots. An unsuccessful tune can still be recorded; starting matrix slots does not add them here.

There are three tabs, no Archive tab. World hides stations marked dead by the health checker; saved snapshots can still appear in Favorites/Recent. These tabs do not require a world-index download, but their streams still need network access. Switching tabs retains the country and search filters. To see saved stations from all countries, use Start > World / clear filters, then switch to Favorites or Recent. Merely switching back to World does not clear a loaded country; the menu action does.

The channel panel has eight rows and truncates list names to 15 characters. The globe's numeral counts loaded country entries, not confirmed live stations or necessarily the number remaining after search/health filters.

Build a watch matrix

Select a station while browsing, then choose Start > Add selected to matrix. Repeat for up to four slots, then choose Watch matrix. Slots are session-only, may contain duplicates, and are separate from favorites. Clear matrix clears the queued slots, not players already running; use Stop playback or B to stop those.

Use the D-pad or L1/R1 to move focus, or point at a cell and press A. Only that cell supplies audio; focusing an empty slot is silent. All populated cells keep decoding independently. There is no per-slot remove/replace or maximize command: clear the queue and rebuild it, or stop and select a station normally for fullscreen. Four-view mode is experimental and may be too demanding for a particular stream combination.

Search

Press X for the Local AI bold keyboard with its cached green cursor. A types, B deletes and closes the keyboard when the draft is empty, Y inserts a space, and X applies the search. Explicit FIND and CLOSE keys are available in the action bar.

Search is a local filter, not worldwide discovery. It matches all typed words, case-insensitively, against station name, country/code, language and tags in the current country list or saved Favorites/Recent snapshots. Available metadata varies by playlist; a language query cannot match missing language fields. The draft is limited to 80 characters. No search text is sent to a remote search service.

Choose countries with Start > Browse countries, not the search keyboard. That picker works before any channels load. With an empty World list, search has nothing to match and does not fetch a catalog. CLEAR only empties the draft; press FIND or X to apply that empty filter. CLOSE discards draft edits. Opening a country resets the query and returns to World; late country responses cannot replace a newer selection.

Downloads and caches

Country-only loading. Normal startup reads bundled geography, saved state and health records; it does not load the old global catalog.json or fetch channel/stream/feed indexes. Opening a country uses https://iptv-org.github.io/iptv/countries/<code>.m3u, parsed into countries/<CODE>.json in the data root. A cache younger than 24 hours is reused. After expiry, or on Refresh selected country, TV attempts a new fetch; a failed fetch can fall back to the existing cache regardless of age. A first-time 404 produces an empty country cache.

Channel selection checks HLS renditions and listed alternatives in the background, preferring a source within the handheld quality target before decoding. Where needed, a private one-rendition master retains separate audio/subtitle groups and is removed when the player closes. Quality changes affect only the focused cell and never saved favorites/history. Unknown dimensions are checked once decoding begins; HD-only streams can still play with a warning and may be slow. This is source selection, not a guarantee that a source under the target exists.

The backend also retains a global-index refresh API using iptv-org channel/stream/feed data, with a master-playlist fallback, but normal country browsing does not call it. The previously recorded 13,498 station/feed entries were a historical global-index observation, not today's shipped channel total. Country playlists change and may have limited metadata, duplicates/alternative sources, dead URLs or regional restrictions. Availability depends on the broadcaster.

Privacy boundaries

The app has no account, project-hosted playback proxy or telemetry service. Country-list requests go to iptv-org's GitHub Pages host over HTTPS. Selecting a station can contact broadcaster/CDN alternatives and HLS playlists before a picture appears; decoding then fetches media, and manual health rechecking also makes HTTP requests. Those hosts see your IP address and normal connection metadata. Streams and their redirects may use HTTP as well as HTTPS; catalog HTTPS does not make every broadcast encrypted or trusted.

Installation/update discovery separately contacts GitHub's release endpoint and asset hosts. Missing dependencies may contact configured APT repositories; a runtime rebuild also has its own source/package downloads. Release archive verification does not authenticate a third-party channel or promise its safety or availability.

Search is local filtering and sends no query to a search service. Favorites, recent choices and preferences are unencrypted local JSON; they are not uploaded by TV, but can identify what you watched. There is no app-level password, encrypted vault, remote wipe or cloud backup. Anyone with access to the data root or backup can read them.

app.log and installer.log can contain stream errors, URLs and system paths. Review them before sharing logs or an SD image. Local is not encrypted.

Owner-verified decoder numbers exist for one handheld: four simultaneous 320x180 test decoders at about 14 frames/s each, including an audio-focus handoff. That was a decoder probe, not a four-station live-stream or matrix-display benchmark, and it is not a speed promise for any network stream.

Known pitfalls

  • Matrix mode is experimental. Treat four-station watching as a probe, not a settled feature.
  • HD-only streams are slow. The warning-and-continue path is intentional; scaling cannot make every HD source smooth on this hardware.
  • Offline browsing is not offline television. Saved favorites/recent and cached country lists can be viewed offline. New lists and live media need a network; demo scenes never tune their sample stations.
  • Signal unavailable means just that. The catalog indexes stations that may be dark; availability is the broadcaster's, not the app's.
  • Never launch TV in parallel with the menu. KMSDRM is not a desktop compositor; a background nohup launch races EmulationStation for rendering and input. The app refuses unowned launches, but two ES sessions or background processes can still collide.
  • The station list is a snapshot. Country playlists refresh on a 24-hour country cache or manual Refresh; freshly open stations can be missing and dark ones can linger.

For install failures use Troubleshooting; for the evidence behind the hardware claims, see Verification.
